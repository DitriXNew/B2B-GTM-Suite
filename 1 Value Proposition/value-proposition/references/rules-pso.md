# PSO (Problem · Solution · Outcome) Rules — Detailed Reference

## PSO (Problem · Solution · Outcome) sentence pattern

PSO (Problem · Solution · Outcome) stands for:
- **P (Problem)** — the concrete pain the customer feels, and what it costs them.
- **S (Solution)** — what you specifically do about it (mechanism, parameter, method).
- **O (Outcome)** — the measurable business result for the customer (EUR, %, time, units).

### Standard version
"Because **[P (Problem)]**, we offer **[S (Solution)]**, which makes it possible to **[O (Outcome)]**."

### Narrative version (preferred)
"Many of our customers [P (Problem) — description of the problem], which is why we [S (Solution) — what we do]. As a result, [O (Outcome) — business result]."

### Punchy version
"We help achieve **[O (Outcome)]** by solving **[P (Problem)]** through **[S (Solution)]**."

## Examples of good PSOs

**Product (packaging producer):**
"Many of our customers see whole batches rejected because the print on the packaging fades during transport, which is why we use UV-cured inks rated for 18 months of outdoor exposure. As a result, the rejection rate on shipped batches dropped from 7% to under 1%, saving them roughly €30,000 a year in scrapped product."

**Product (industrial pump supplier):**
"Many plants have to shut down a line for half a day just to swap a worn pump seal, because standard units aren't built for quick service. Our pumps use a cartridge seal that one technician replaces in 15 minutes without dismantling the housing. As a result, an unplanned stop costs 15 minutes instead of 4 hours, and at €2,000/h of downtime, that's €7,700 saved per failure."

**Service value (electronics contract manufacturer):**
"Customers are often afraid to commit to a large run before they know the design holds up in the field. That's why we produce a free 50-unit pilot batch and run it through full functional testing before mass production. As a result, design faults are caught before scale-up, and the average customer avoids one costly recall worth €60,000–120,000."

**Service value (logistics provider):**
"Many of our customers can't tell their own clients when a shipment will actually arrive, because their previous carrier only updated status once a day. That's why we give every customer a live tracking portal with GPS updates every 10 minutes and an ETA accurate to 30 minutes. As a result, their customer-service team handles 40% fewer 'where is my order' calls."

**Added (chemical distributor):**
"Many of our customers are small workshops without a compliance officer, and they struggle to keep up with changing REACH and safety-data regulations. That's why we send updated safety data sheets and regulatory alerts the moment a rule changes. As a result, the customer passes audits without scrambling and avoids fines that can reach €15,000 per violation."

**Added (CNC tooling supplier):**
"Customers often run their tools at the wrong feed and speed settings, which wears them out twice as fast and drives up cost-per-part. That's why our engineer provides a free cutting-parameter sheet tailored to each customer's material and machine. As a result, tool life increased by 60% and the customer's tooling spend dropped by about €18,000 a year."

## Examples of BAD values (anti-patterns)

### Empty slogans (no PSO (Problem · Solution · Outcome))
- "We have our own production facility." -> No P (Problem) and no O (Outcome). So what does that change for the customer?
- "We are a trusted partner." -> No P (Problem) and no O (Outcome). Trusted by whom, and what does that deliver?
- "We use only certified materials." -> Which certificate, and what problem does it prevent?
- "We are passionate about what we do." -> Empty. Zero information.
- "We offer competitive prices." -> Compared to whom? By how much? What does the customer gain?
- "We have years of experience." -> And what does that experience give the customer?
- "Quality is our top priority." -> Every competitor says this. Which parameter, which standard, how is it measured?
- "We put the customer first." -> A claim, not a value. What do you actually do that proves it?
- "We deliver tailor-made solutions." -> Tailored how? What problem with off-the-shelf does that fix, and what's the result?
- "We are a leader in the market." -> Leader by what metric? And what does that give the customer specifically?
- "We are a modern, dynamic company." -> Adjectives about you, nothing about the customer's problem or outcome.
- "We guarantee satisfaction." -> Guaranteed how? What happens if they're not satisfied, and what risk does that remove?

### False PSOs (no real problem or outcome)
"Customers want fast service, we are fast, so the customer gets fast service."
-> P (Problem) repeats S (Solution), O (Outcome) repeats S (Solution). Zero substance.

"Companies need an innovative partner, we are innovative, so the customer has innovation."
-> The same word three times. No specifics.

"Customers need quality, we deliver quality, so the customer has quality."
-> Circular. No real problem, no measurable outcome.

"Businesses want to save money, we are cost-effective, so the customer saves money."
-> P (Problem), S (Solution) and O (Outcome) are all the same idea restated. Save how much? Compared to what?

"Clients value reliability, we are reliable, so our clients can rely on us."
-> Restates the same word. No mechanism, no number.

### Fake problem (the "problem" is just wanting the product)
- "Customers need good packaging, so we provide good packaging." -> "Needing the product" isn't a problem. What goes *wrong* without it: rejected batches, returns, lost sales?
- "Companies want reliable suppliers, so we are a reliable supplier." -> What does unreliability actually cost them: downtime, missed deadlines, penalties?
- "Customers are looking for a partner they can trust." -> Vague desire, not a concrete pain. What bad experience are they trying to avoid?

### Outcome that benefits you, not the customer
- "increases our sales" -> That's *your* outcome. What does the customer get?
- "lets us scale faster" -> Your benefit. Reframe to the customer's saving/gain.
- "strengthens our market position" -> About you. What problem does it solve for them?
- "builds long-term relationships" -> Nice for you. What measurable result does the customer see?

### Outcome without a number (needs improvement)
- "improves energy efficiency" -> By how much? What % less power, how much per year (EUR)?
- "extends equipment lifespan" -> By how many years? From how many to how many?
- "speeds up order processing" -> By how much? From how many hours to how many?
- "reduces material waste" -> From what % to what? How much per month?

With every qualitative outcome, ask: "Can you estimate a number? E.g. % of savings, EUR, days, units?" Accept a description ONLY when the user confirms there is no number.

### Typical, banal values (reject or dig deeper)
- "wide product range" -> How many SKUs? Wider than whom? What problem does it solve?
- "customer-oriented service" -> Oriented how, specifically?
- "qualified specialists" -> Qualified in what? What certifications? What does it deliver?
- "comprehensive solutions" -> Comprehensive how? What exactly is included that others skip?
- "reliable products" -> Which parameter proves it? Which standard? How many % fewer failures?
- "innovative approach" -> What specifically is innovative, and what does it give the customer?

**Rule:** If a value sounds like a line from a marketing brochure, dig deeper or reject it.

## The non-obviousness rule

A list of values is worthless if it's made up only of generic values. The most valuable values are:

- **Atypical** — competitors don't offer them
- **Specific** — backed by numbers, names, parameters
- **Rare** — hard to copy

Generic values (speed, quality, flexibility) belong on the list ONLY when they're backed by specifics and numbers, e.g. "we deliver within 4h within a 100km radius" instead of "fast deliveries".

## Checklist of areas to review

When you're short of values to reach 20 (or hunting for more to reach 30+), work through the areas below and ask about the ones that haven't been covered:

### Product
- [ ] Technical parameters / distinguishing specification
- [ ] Material / raw material / composition — better than standard
- [ ] Durability / lifespan / reliability (MTBF, technical warranty)
- [ ] Performance / speed / precision
- [ ] Scope of the service vs the competition (what's in the package that others don't have)
- [ ] Product certifications / standards / approvals (CE, ISO, industry-specific)
- [ ] Personalization / configurability / made-to-order production
- [ ] Compatibility / integration with the customer's other systems
- [ ] Assortment / breadth of offer / variants

### Service
- [ ] Delivery time and logistics (warehouse, transport, availability)
- [ ] Logistics minimum / order size
- [ ] Ordering process (methods, convenience, repeatability)
- [ ] Response time to tickets / after-sales service / SLA
- [ ] Onboarding / implementation / go-live
- [ ] Dedicated account manager / technical advisor
- [ ] Contract terms (flexibility, termination)
- [ ] Payment terms / financing / trade credit
- [ ] Reporting and transparency
- [ ] Complaints and warranty — how fast and how smooth
- [ ] What happens after the engagement ends / offboarding / continuity

### Added
- [ ] Technical advisory / audits / solution selection
- [ ] Customer education (training, content, webinars)
- [ ] Community / networking between customers
- [ ] Partnerships and integrations that deliver extra benefits
- [ ] Brand / recognition as social proof
- [ ] Location / proximity / geographic availability
- [ ] Industry know-how that goes beyond the scope of the product/service
- [ ] References / case studies / samples
- [ ] Help with securing grants / certifications / documentation

## Value-extracting questions

### About the product/service
- What do you do faster than the competition?
- What do you do more accurately?
- What do you do better?
- Which of the things you do reduces the customer's risk?
- Which product parameter matters most to customers, and how do you measure up?
- What material / technology do you use, and why is it better?
- What certifications does the product carry, and what do they give the customer?
- Do you produce to order / personalize? What does that look like?

### About the service experience
- Where do you exceed the customer's expectations?
- What does the ordering / implementation process look like step by step?
- What happens when something goes wrong? How fast do you respond?
- What are customers afraid of before buying, and how do you address it?
- What are your payment / warranty / SLA terms?
- What does progress reporting look like: what does the customer get, and how often?
- What happens when the engagement ends? What does the handover look like?
- What does logistics look like: warehouse, delivery, minimum order?
- How do you handle complaints?

### About added values
- What does the customer get beyond the product/service itself?
- What do customers thank you for that they didn't expect?
- What do customers say when you ask them "why do you work with us"?
- Do you do anything that educates customers or builds their competencies?
- Do you have partnerships / integrations that give the customer extra value?
- Do you help customers with something you don't charge for?

### Number-probing (use with EVERY outcome)
- How much exactly is it? (EUR, %, days, units, hours)
- Can you give a range? (e.g. "20-30% faster")
- How does it compare numerically with the competition?
- What's the financial effect for the customer: how much do they save / earn?
- Do you have a customer example with specific numbers?

---

## Rule: it is NOT a value if...

- It doesn't solve any customer problem
- It doesn't deliver any business outcome
- Every company in the industry can say the same thing
- It can't be verified (no specifics)

When the user proposes something like this, don't reject it outright, but dig deeper: "That's interesting. And what specific customer problem does it solve? And what effect does it have on their business?"

## Deduplication examples

### Duplicate — merge:
- "Fast delivery" + "Large warehouse ensuring availability" -> the same mechanism (warehouse) solving the same problem (the customer is waiting for goods). Merge into one value.
- "Flexible payment terms" + "Long 60-day payment term" -> one is a subset of the other. Merge.
- "24/7 technical support" + "We always pick up the phone" -> the same mechanism (round-the-clock availability) solving the same problem (the customer is stuck and can't reach anyone). Merge.
- "Free delivery" + "No extra logistics charges" -> two phrasings of the same benefit (the customer pays nothing for transport). Merge.
- "Custom-made to order" + "We adapt the product to your specs" -> the same mechanism (made-to-order production) solving the same problem (off-the-shelf doesn't fit). Merge.
- "Dedicated account manager" + "One point of contact for everything" -> the same mechanism (a single named contact) solving the same problem (the customer is bounced between departments). Merge.

### NOT a duplicate — keep separate:
- "Large warehouse -> the customer doesn't have to wait for delivery" (problem: production downtime) vs "Large warehouse -> the customer doesn't have to keep their own stock" (problem: frozen capital) -> DIFFERENT customer problems, so these are two separate values.
- "Fast response to tickets -> minimizes downtime during a breakdown" (problem: line is stopped now) vs "Preventive maintenance plan -> fewer breakdowns happen at all" (problem: failures occur in the first place) -> one is reactive, one is preventive. DIFFERENT problems, keep separate.
- "ISO certification -> the customer passes their own audits" (problem: compliance risk) vs "ISO certification -> guarantees consistent quality batch to batch" (problem: product variability) -> same mechanism, but DIFFERENT customer problems. Keep separate.
- "30-day return policy -> lowers the risk of a wrong purchase" (problem: buyer's risk before the sale) vs "Fast complaint handling -> minimizes loss when a defect appears" (problem: dealing with a defect after the sale) -> DIFFERENT stages and problems. Keep separate.

## Output file format

### File 1: `value-propositions.md` (Markdown — full documentation)

Structure:
```markdown
# Value Propositions — [Company name]
Date: [date]
Model: Ananas-Agency PSO

## Product Values
### PV-01. [Value title] — Score: X/20
- **Problem:** [P]
- **Solution:** [S]
- **Outcome:** [O]
- **Source:** hypothesis
> "[Complete PSO sentence]"

The ID prefix matches the category: PV- for Product value, SV- for Service value, AV- for Added value.
`Source:` is `hypothesis` until a Persona Experiment (Skill 4) tests the value; it then
becomes `validated` (or stays `hypothesis` if refuted). New values start as `hypothesis`.

## Service Values
[...]

## Added Values
[...]

## Summary
- Total number of values: XX
- Top 5 strongest (cross-category): [list, cited by ID]

## Competitor snapshot
Which competitors also claim each value (✓ = claims it, ✗ = doesn't, ? = unsure).
A value is `unique` only when the whole row is ✗.

| Value ID | [Competitor A] | [Competitor B] | [Competitor C] | Unique? |
|----------|----------------|----------------|----------------|---------|
| PV-01    | ✗              | ✗              | ?              | no      |
| SV-02    | ✗              | ✗              | ✗              | **yes** |
```

### File 2: `value-propositions.html` (styled, share-ready)

A single **self-contained** `.html` file (all CSS inline in one `<style>` block, **no external assets and no JavaScript**), so it opens in any browser, prints cleanly to PDF, and can be emailed as one file. Render the **same content** as the Markdown version, mapped onto the components below. Keep the `<style>` block **identical across every skill in this suite** so all deliverables share one look (this is the shared design system referenced by the other skills).

- One `<section class="block">` per category, in order: Product → Service → Added.
- One `.card` per value (the section it sits in carries the category: no per-card colour coding).
- In each card header: the `.id` chip (PV-/SV-/AV-), the score as a mini meter — `<span class="scorewrap"><span class="meter"><i style="width:NN%"></i></span><span class="scoreno">X/20</span></span>` (bar width = score/20), the `.tag-unique` chip **only** for unique values, and a **`Source` pill** — `<span class="tag validated">validated</span>` or `<span class="tag hypothesis">hypothesis</span>` (yellow = untested, green = confirmed).
- Problem / Solution / Outcome as `.row`s; the full PSO (Problem · Solution · Outcome) sentence in a `<blockquote>`.
- A **Summary block** (total count + top-5 by ID).
- Close with a **Competitor snapshot block** — a `<table>` (values × competitors, ✓/✗/? cells, Unique? column), matching the `.md` snapshot.
